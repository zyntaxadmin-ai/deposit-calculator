Florida Security Deposit Law: 15 Days, 30 Days and the 15-Day Objection

Florida's deposit law runs on two clocks and one very short objection window. Miss any of them and you or your landlord can lose rights.

Updated November 18, 2026 · 3 min read · General information, not legal advice

Key takeaways

  • If the landlord isn't making a claim, the deposit is due within 15 days after the tenancy ends.
  • If the landlord wants to keep something, they must send a notice by certified mail within 30 days stating the amount and the reason.
  • You then have 15 days after receiving the notice to object in writing. Don't miss this window.
  • A landlord who misses the 30-day notice forfeits the right to impose a claim on the deposit, and the prevailing party in a dispute gets court costs and reasonable attorney's fees.

StatuteFla. Stat. § 83.49

Florida's rules are in Florida Statutes section 83.49. The process has two tracks, depending on whether the landlord plans to keep any of your deposit.

Track one: no claim

If the landlord isn't making a claim against the deposit, it must be returned within 15 days after the termination of the rental agreement.

Track two: the landlord wants to keep something

If the landlord intends to impose a claim, they must give written notice within 30 days after termination. The notice must be sent by certified mail to your last known mailing address (or by email under section 83.505, where that applies) and must state the amount of the claim and the reason for it. A landlord who doesn't send a timely notice forfeits the right to impose a claim on the deposit, although they may still sue for damages after returning it.

Your 15-day window to object

You have 15 days after you receive the landlord's notice of intention to impose a claim to object in writing. If you object, the landlord cannot simply deduct the amount based on the notice alone, and either side may sue to determine who is entitled to the money. If you don't object in time, the landlord can generally deduct the claimed amount. Treat this as the most time-sensitive step in the process.

Object in writing, and keep proof

Send your objection by email and certified mail, state which charges you dispute and why, and keep copies. Calendar the day you received the notice, because the 15 days run from receipt.

Attorney's fees can go both ways

In an action over the deposit, the prevailing party is entitled to court costs and a reasonable attorney's fee. That cuts both ways: it can help a tenant with a strong case, and it can be costly if you lose. Check your facts and evidence before you file.

Interest and how the deposit is held

A Florida landlord must tell you in writing at the start of the tenancy how the deposit is held. The landlord may hold it in a non-interest-bearing account (no interest is required), in an interest-bearing account (you receive at least 75% of the annualized average rate or 5% simple interest, whichever the landlord elects), or may post a surety bond (you receive 5% simple interest per year). Interest, if owed, must be paid at least once a year, directly or as a credit against rent.

If you leave early

If you vacate before the end of the term, the statute asks you to give the landlord at least seven days' written notice, by certified mail or personal delivery, including an address where you can be reached. Failing to give it doesn't waive your right to the deposit, but it can relieve the landlord of the 30-day notice requirement.

Steps for Florida tenants

  1. Send your new address in writing when you move out, by email and certified mail.
  2. Take dated photos and video of the unit.
  3. Count 15 days from termination. If no notice or refund arrives, send a demand letter.
  4. If you receive a notice of claim, object in writing within 15 days of receipt.
  5. If the dispute continues, consider small claims. See our guide.

Quick answers

What if the landlord misses the 30-day notice?

The landlord forfeits the right to impose a claim on the deposit and cannot set it off, though they may still sue for damages after returning the deposit.

How should the landlord's notice be sent?

By certified mail to your last known mailing address, or by email under section 83.505 where it applies, and it must state the amount and reason for the claim.

Does Florida require landlords to pay interest on deposits?

Not always. If the landlord keeps the deposit in a non-interest-bearing account, no interest is required. Other arrangements carry interest, as described above.
