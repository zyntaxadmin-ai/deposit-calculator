How to Write a Security Deposit Demand Letter (What to Include)

A good demand letter is short, factual and impossible to misunderstand. It often gets landlords to pay, and if it doesn't, it becomes the first exhibit in your small claims case.

Updated November 18, 2026 · 3 min read · General information, not legal advice

Key takeaways

  • State the facts, the amount you're owed, the legal basis and a firm but reasonable response date.
  • Send it in a way you can prove, such as certified mail with a return receipt, and keep a copy.
  • Stay calm and specific. Insults and threats weaken your position.

Many landlords don't return deposits on time because they assume tenants will give up. A clear, written demand shows you know your rights and are willing to enforce them, and it creates a paper trail a judge can read in one minute.

When to send it

Send the letter after your state's return deadline has passed or after you receive an itemized statement you believe is wrong. If the deadline hasn't passed yet, the landlord technically still has time, so a letter then is just a polite reminder. Our calculator shows how many days remain.

What to include

  1. Your name and the date. Use your current mailing address.
  2. The landlord's name and address. Use the person or company named in your lease or on your rent receipts.
  3. The rental address and key dates. The move-out date, the date you returned keys, and when you sent your forwarding address.
  4. The deposit amount you paid and what you've received back, if anything.
  5. The deadline and the law. Name your state's statute and the date by which the landlord was required to act.
  6. What you are asking for. The exact dollar amount, and any penalty your state allows if you're claiming it.
  7. A response date. Ten days is common. It gives the landlord a fair chance and you a clear next step.
  8. The next step. State plainly that you may file in small claims court if you don't hear back.
  9. A forwarding address for payment.

A simple structure

Re: Return of security deposit, [rental address]

Dear [Landlord],

I moved out of the property above on [date]. I paid a security deposit of $[amount]. Under [state statute], it was due back, or an itemized list of deductions was due, by [deadline].

As of today I have not received $[amount owed]. I am requesting that amount [plus the statutory penalty of $[amount] under [statute], for a total of $[total]].

Please pay within [10] days, by [date]. My forwarding address is [address].

If I do not receive payment, I may file a claim in small claims court and seek all remedies the law allows.

Sincerely,
[Your name]

How to send it

  • Certified mail with return receipt gives you proof of delivery. Keep the receipt and tracking number.
  • Also email or text it if the landlord communicates that way. Some states treat electronic notice as valid, but check yours.
  • Keep a copy of the letter exactly as sent, and photograph the envelope and receipt.
What to leave out

Skip insults, threats of things you won't do, and unrelated complaints about the apartment. Don't include private details like medical information. A short, factual letter is more persuasive than a long angry one.

Include a penalty only if your state allows it

Penalties depend on state law and often on the landlord's conduct. In Texas, for example, a landlord who keeps a deposit in bad faith can owe $100 plus three times the amount wrongfully withheld, plus attorney's fees. In California, a court may award up to twice the deposit for bad-faith retention. Our calculator shows an upper-end estimate; remember that a court decides what is actually awarded.

If they still don't pay

Your letter, receipts, photos and calendar notes are the foundation of a small claims case. Mark your response deadline in your calendar, and file if it passes.

Quick answers

Is a demand letter required before suing?

Not always, but it's strongly recommended, and some courts and states expect it. It shows you gave the landlord a chance to fix the problem and documents the dates.

Can I send the letter by email?

You can, and you should keep a copy, but also send it by certified mail where possible. Proof of delivery matters if the case goes to court.

What if I don't know my landlord's current address?

Use the address on your lease, rent receipts or the property manager's website, and send copies to any other address you have. Keep proof of every attempt.
