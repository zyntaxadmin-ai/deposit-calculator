Washington Security Deposit Law: 30 Days, Move-In Checklists and Penalties

Washington landlords must postmark a refund or a written statement within 30 days. Miss it, and they owe the full deposit and can't argue deductions in court.

Updated November 18, 2026 · 3 min read · General information, not legal advice

Key takeaways

  • A landlord has 30 days after the tenancy ends and you vacate to deliver or postmark a written statement of why they're keeping any deposit, plus any refund.
  • A landlord who misses the deadline is liable for the full deposit and is barred from asserting any claim to keep any part of it in your lawsuit.
  • A court may award up to twice the deposit for intentional refusal, and the prevailing party may recover attorney's fees.
  • The landlord must give a written move-in condition statement. Without it, the landlord can be liable for the deposit.

Washington's rules are in the Residential Landlord-Tenant Act, mainly RCW 59.18.260 (move-in condition) and RCW 59.18.280 (return of deposits).

The 30-day deadline

Within 30 days after the rental agreement ends and you've vacated, the landlord must deliver a statement of the basis for retaining any part of the deposit, plus any refund owed. If you abandon the unit, the 30 days run from when the landlord learns of the abandonment. Delivery can be personal, or by first-class mail to your last known address, postmarked within the 30 days. Washington's statute refers to your "last known address" rather than requiring a forwarding address, but tenant advocates recommend that you provide one in writing and keep proof, since that makes delivery clear.

What happens if the landlord is late

A landlord who fails to provide the statement, any required documentation and the refund within the deadline is liable for the full deposit amount. In a tenant's action to recover the deposit, they are also barred from asserting any claim or defense for keeping any part of it. There are exceptions if circumstances beyond the landlord's control prevented compliance or if the tenant abandoned the premises.

Penalties for intentional refusal

A court may, in its discretion, award up to two times the amount of the deposit when a landlord intentionally refuses to provide the statement, documentation or refund. In an action to recover the deposit, the prevailing party may recover the cost of suit or arbitration, including reasonable attorney's fees.

The move-in condition statement

RCW 59.18.260 requires a landlord who collects a deposit to give you a written statement describing the condition of the unit at move-in, which you and the landlord sign. According to the Tenants Union of Washington, a landlord who collects a deposit without providing that written statement can be liable to the tenant for the deposit amount. If you never received one, mention it in your demand letter.

Normal wear and tear

You are expected to return the unit in its move-in condition, minus normal wear and tear. There's no precise legal standard for what counts as normal wear and tear, so photographs of the condition when you moved in and out are your best protection. Read our guide.

Postmark matters

Because the statute is satisfied by mailing within 30 days, the date on the postmark is what counts, not when you receive the envelope. Keep the envelope if the statement arrives late.

Steps for Washington tenants

  1. Locate your move-in condition statement and photos.
  2. Take dated photos and video at move-out.
  3. Send your forwarding address in writing.
  4. Calendar day 30 after you vacate.
  5. If nothing is postmarked in time, send a demand letter citing RCW 59.18.280.
  6. If needed, file in small claims. See our guide.

Quick answers

Does the landlord have to give me a forwarding-address form?

The statute refers to your last known address. Still, send your new address in writing and keep proof, so mail goes to the right place.

Can a landlord keep my deposit if they miss the 30 days?

They owe the full deposit and are barred from asserting a claim to keep any part of it in your action, subject to narrow exceptions.

Is the double-damages penalty automatic?

No. A court may, in its discretion, award up to two times the deposit for intentional refusal.
