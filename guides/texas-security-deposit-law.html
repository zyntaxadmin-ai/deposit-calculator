Texas Security Deposit Law: The 30-Day Rule and Bad-Faith Penalties

Texas gives landlords 30 days, but it also puts a few traps in the tenant's path. Know the forwarding address rule, the lease notice rule and the penalties before you move out.

Updated November 18, 2026 · 3 min read · General information, not legal advice

Key takeaways

  • A landlord must refund your deposit, or send an itemized list of deductions, on or before the 30th day after you surrender the premises.
  • The landlord isn't required to refund the deposit or send the list until you give a written forwarding address, though you don't lose your right to the money by failing to.
  • A landlord who keeps a deposit in bad faith can owe $100 plus three times the amount wrongfully withheld plus attorney's fees.
  • Never withhold your last month's rent to cover the deposit. Texas law can make that expensive.

Texas deposits are governed by Subchapter C of Chapter 92 of the Property Code. The framework is simple, but the details matter, especially for tenants.

The 30-day rule

Under section 92.103, a landlord must refund the security deposit on or before the 30th day after the tenant surrenders the premises. In practice, if the landlord is keeping anything, they must return the balance along with a written description and itemized list of all deductions (section 92.104). The itemized list isn't required if you owe rent at surrender and there's no dispute about the amount.

Give a written forwarding address

Section 92.107 says the landlord is not obligated to return your deposit or give you the written description of damages and charges until you give a written statement of your forwarding address for the purpose of refunding the deposit. The same section says you don't forfeit your right to a refund or to the description merely because you failed to give an address. In other words, the missing address delays the landlord's duty but doesn't erase your rights. Don't take the chance; send your address in writing on move-out day.

The advance-notice trap

Some leases say you must give advance notice of move-out to get your deposit back. Under section 92.103(b), that requirement is effective only if it is underlined or printed in conspicuous bold print in the lease. Read your lease carefully, and give notice either way.

What a landlord can and can't keep

A landlord may subtract damages and charges for which the tenant is legally liable under the lease, or that result from the tenant breaching it. Section 92.104 is explicit that a landlord may not retain any portion of a security deposit to cover normal wear and tear. See our guide to wear and tear vs. damage.

Texas doesn't set a statewide maximum deposit, so check your lease.

Bad faith, penalties and presumptions

Section 92.109 sets out the consequences when a landlord wrongfully keeps your money:

  • A landlord who retains a deposit in bad faith is liable for $100, plus three times the portion of the deposit wrongfully withheld, plus the tenant's reasonable attorney's fees in a suit to recover the deposit.
  • A landlord who in bad faith fails to provide a written itemized list of deductions loses the right to keep any part of the deposit or to sue the tenant for damages to the premises, and also owes the tenant's reasonable attorney's fees.
  • A landlord who doesn't return the deposit or provide the itemized description by the 30th day is presumed to have acted in bad faith.
  • In a tenant's suit, the landlord has the burden of proving that keeping any part of the deposit was reasonable.

Those provisions give tenants real leverage. A short, factual demand letter that cites them often gets a response.

Don't withhold your last month's rent

Section 92.108 says a tenant may not withhold any portion of the last month's rent on the grounds that the security deposit covers it. A tenant who does so is presumed to act in bad faith and can owe three times the rent wrongfully withheld, plus the landlord's attorney's fees in a suit to recover it.

Steps for Texas tenants

  1. Check the lease for a move-out notice requirement, and give written notice.
  2. Take dated photos and video of the whole unit.
  3. Send your forwarding address in writing and keep proof.
  4. Calendar day 30 after you surrender the premises. Our calculator does the math.
  5. If nothing arrives, send a demand letter with a response date.
  6. If needed, file in justice court, Texas's small claims court. See our small claims guide.

Quick answers

What if I never gave a forwarding address?

The landlord's duty to refund and to send the itemized list doesn't start until you give a written forwarding address, but you don't forfeit the refund. Send the address now, in writing.

Is there a limit on how much a Texas landlord can charge as a deposit?

Texas has no statewide cap, so the amount is set by the lease.

Does the landlord have to prove my deductions were reasonable?

Yes. In a tenant's suit under the deposit subchapter, the landlord has the burden of proving that retaining any portion of the deposit was reasonable.
